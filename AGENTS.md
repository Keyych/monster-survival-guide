# Monster illustration style memory

Apply this direction whenever creating or replacing a monster drawing.
The user established it on 2026-10-20.

## Visual references

Use the **original eight published drawings** in `src/assets/illustrations/`
as the visual standard: `wendigo.png`, `slenderman.png`,
`black-eyed-children.png`, `mothman.png`, `beast-of-bray-road.png`,
`jersey-devil.png`, `skinwalker.png`, and `the-rake.png`.
Inspect them and supply relevant originals as image-generation style references.
Do not reinterpret the style from the word “rough” alone.

## Required character

- A writer's hand-drawn black-ink doodle inside a monster journal.
- Keep recognizable anatomy, distinguishing features, and enough internal
  detail to match the original eight. **Rough does not mean simplistic,
  barely visible, or an outline icon.**
- Use thin, scratchy, broken and overlapping pen strokes, searching contours,
  small corrections, and unfinished edges that fade into disconnected marks.
- Retain texture and form through selective linework. Shading is light,
  scattered, and limited: a little open hatching, with generous unmarked areas.
  Avoid dense hatching, heavy shadow masses, smoothly shaded faces, or filled
  hair and bodies.
- Keep the drawing substantial and legible at the site's actual display size.
  Match the originals' framing and scale, usually a partial figure or study.
- Use a genuinely transparent PNG, including unmarked areas inside the figure,
  so the notebook paper shows through. Black ink only.
- No color rendering, polished final inking, vector-clean contours, photorealism,
  painted grayscale, paper baked into the image, borders, or sticker outlines.
- No titles, captions, labels, signatures, or text on or beside the artwork.
  Descriptive accessible alt text is appropriate.

## Review

Compare new drawings side by side with the original eight on the notebook's
paper background and at their actual page size. Check detail, line weight,
roughness, and shading together. Do not solve excess shading by deleting the
creature's features or reducing it to a handful of faint lines.

Keep the page's notebook character and uncaptioned doodles when integrating
artwork. Source-context notes belong to the entry's text, not an artwork title.
